# E1 — Population fill-policy divergence

**Version 1.0 · 2026-08-30.** Cite as *"Plutus E1 Fill-Divergence table"*.
Regenerated by `measurements/fill_divergence.py` (reproduce step `[11/11]`); the
numbers below trace to `figures/e1_fill_divergence.json`.

The paper's fill-sensitivity result, over a **realistic order flow** rather than
three hand-written strategies. Five on-grid order intents per HSX-stock ticker-day
in 2022 (post the 2021-01-04 lot change, so one undated `HSX_EXCHANGE` is correct),
each evaluated under `soft` / `hard` / `probabilistic` — **477,537 sign-free
questions across 385 tickers × ≥200 sessions.** Reported as fills and executed
quantity, **never P&L** (`DivergenceReport`'s own contract). The three policies
agree on **68.4%** of decisions — so **31.6% of a realistic order flow has its
outcome set by the fill assumption, not the market.**

## Fill rate by order intent and policy

*Share of that order type that fills. The policies are an assumption ladder,
left to right: `hard` fills only what it can prove (a trade strictly through the
price); `soft` assumes every touch fills; `probabilistic` assumes each touch with
a coin-flip (`p_touch = 0.5`), landing about halfway between. The `hard`→`soft`
gap is pure assumption.*

| Order intent | `hard` — data-proven | `probabilistic` | `soft` — assumes every touch |
|---|--:|--:|--:|
| **Market order** (take liquidity) | 0% | 0% | **89%** |
| **Limit at the market price** | **73%** | 81% | 89% |
| **Limit far from market** (band edge) | 0% | 4% | 8% |

Read the rows, not a single number:

- **Market order → 0% / 0% / 89%.** No policy can *prove* a market-order fill from a
  daily bar; only `soft` fills it, and only by assuming. `hard` and `probabilistic`
  decline. A market-order fill is **pure assumption**.
- **Limit at the market price → 73% / 81% / 89%.** Mostly *data-proven* (73% trade
  through); `soft` adds 16 points of assumption on top, `probabilistic` about half
  of that.
- **Limit far from market → 0% / 4% / 8%.** Barely fills under any policy — whether
  the day reached the band edge is a **market fact**, not an assumption. The market
  decides.

So exposure to the fill assumption is a **function of the order mix**: aggressive
orders, the policy decides; passive orders, the market does.

## Executed quantity and abstention, by policy

*The same disagreement in shares. `soft` fills 1.9× the quantity `hard` does. The
indeterminate share is each policy's honest "could not decide" — `hard`, which
refuses to assume, abstains on a third of the flow.*

| Policy | Total shares filled | Indeterminate (abstained) |
|---|--:|--:|
| `soft` | **246M** | 3% |
| `probabilistic` | 148M | 21% |
| `hard` | **128M** | 33% |

## Notes

- **Population.** HSX `instrumenttype='stock'` ticker-days, 2022-01-04..2022-12-30;
  385 tickers with ≥200 sessions; five intents each (a market buy; a limit buy and
  a limit sell at the day's close; a limit buy at the floor; a limit sell at the
  ceiling), all priced off the corpus's own on-grid values so tick-grid rejection
  never confounds the comparison.
- **Policies.** `soft` / `hard` / `probabilistic`, each at the same 10%
  participation cap as the S7 strategy arm, so the result is comparable. Distinct
  signatures, so `compare_policies` keeps them as separate columns.
- **Requires** the daily Parquet corpus with the day's high/low served (the
  `DataHubSource` OHLC wiring of 2026-08-30 — without it every limit is
  INDETERMINATE for the wrong reason).
- **Supersedes** the old E1 taker panel (three strategies × three fill policies,
  reporting return), which was sign-confounded, rested on 4–5 fills, and reported
  P&L. The Sx strategies stay in §V as fidelity evidence; this is the headline
  sensitivity measurement. See `EXPERIMENT-E1-FILL-DIVERGENCE.md`.
