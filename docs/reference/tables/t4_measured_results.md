# T4 — Measured results, each under the policy that produced it

Every headline number the paper reports, with the **fill policy**, **resolution**, and **assumption set** it was measured under, its 95% interval, and the measurement that regenerates it. This is the table form of the paper's central claim: a simulator's outputs are not single numbers but *functions of the modelling choices*, so a result quoted without its policy, resolution, and assumptions is not reproducible. Rows are grouped by contribution; within a group, the columns say exactly what changed between rows and the value column says what that change did.

> **Quote the conditional value, never a group's spread as if it were one number.** The point of a group is that the rows *disagree* — that is the finding. "The maker fill is ~138k shares" is false in the way this table exists to prevent; "the maker fill is 152.6k under an optimistic queue and 123.7k under a conservative one, a 18.9% swing the corpus cannot adjudicate" is the result.

*Source: regenerated by the named module in each row (all under `measurements/` except the F3 strategy rows, which are `strategies/test_f3_cross_policy.py`). Every value in this table was reproduced against the `hermes-parquet` corpus (and, for the tick rows, the `hermes-dev-extract` `local_quote` book) on the machine this was written for; proportions carry a 95% Wilson interval, counts are exact. Corpus window: daily bars 2021-02-05..2022-12-30; the tick contrast is 66 HSX instrument-days, 2022-10-20..2022-11-30.*

## Fill policy — population divergence (E1)

The headline fill-sensitivity result, over a **realistic order flow** rather than three hand-written strategies. ## How to read this table

- **Every group is a disagreement, on purpose.** F2's three rows, F3's fill axis, F3's queue axis, and the two admission rows are all the *same thing measured under different choices*. The paper's contribution is that the choice moves the answer by a material, quantified amount — not any single row.
- **CIs bound sampling noise, not model error.** The Wilson intervals say the *rate* is pinned given the sample; they say nothing about whether the modelling assumption is right. The regime-split and maker-fill swings are the assumption-error axis, and they dwarf the intervals.
- **The tick rows are a narrow population by design.** 66 HSX instrument-days (FPT, HPG, HTV, late 2022) — the only HSX equity names in the `local_quote` book — is a methodological contrast, not a liquidity study; 66 days × a three-order probe is thousands of fill evaluations, ample for the rate.
- **`đ` is Vietnamese đồng, unscaled.** The margin requirements are per-position means over the 381-entry book.
